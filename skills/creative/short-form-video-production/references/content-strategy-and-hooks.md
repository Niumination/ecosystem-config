# Content strategy, formats, and hooks (pre-production)

Everything here is **input to the script**, decided before a composition is authored: what concept the
piece uses, what the hook says, how long it runs, and what the call-to-action asks for. It is research
depth, not a rulebook — **re-verify before use**. Format trends turn over weekly and the platform
numbers below are directional, from third-party analysis, not from this account's own data.

Research **outside** the ecosystem as well as inside it. An idea list built only from our own repos is
a catalogue, not a strategy.

## 1 · What the platform rewards

- **Sends (DM shares) outrank likes.** Ranking weighs watch time, likes-per-reach and sends, with sends
  carrying the most weight for reaching non-followers — estimated several times a like. Practical reading:
  ten shares beat a hundred likes. Design for "someone will send this to a friend", not for admiration.
  What gets sent: resources people can use (a list, a template, a how-to), takes that are recognisably
  *them*, and niche humour.
- **Format split — reach vs depth.** Carousels lead engagement per follower; Reels lead reach. They are
  not competing: Reels to be found, carousels to be remembered. Keep Reels under ~90 s.
- **Hashtags no longer carry discovery.** Followers of new hashtags cannot be acquired; discovery runs on
  caption keywords. Use a handful of hashtags at most and put the real terms in the caption sentences.
- **Polish is penalised relative to authenticity.** Over-produced footage is deprioritised as
  AI-generated material floods the platform; plain, direct, self-shot delivery is favoured. See §8 for how
  this sits against our own design rules.

## 2 · The hook decides everything that follows

A large majority of viewers decide within the first three seconds — the decision itself lands around
1.7 s. A strong opening multiplies engagement several times over versus a weak one; the difference
between a mediocre and a large result often comes down to the opening line alone.

Three triggers, and the strongest hooks combine two of them:

- **Curiosity** — an unfinished loop ("I tested this for 30 days and…")
- **Recognition** — naming the viewer's own situation ("POV: you have been doing this wrong")
- **Surprise or urgency** — a direct interruption ("Stop doing this if…")

Practical requirements: state a payoff promise by ~3 s, make the hook **readable with sound off**, and
kill any intro. Start on a mistake, an outcome, a number, or a question.

## 3 · Structure

```
HOOK      0–3 s    Stop the scroll. One idea, one sentence.
PROBLEM   3–10 s   Name the pain the viewer already feels.
SOLUTION  10–20 s  Present the answer. Do not open with the brand name.
PROOF     20–25 s  One specific result. A number beats an adjective.
CTA       last 3–5 s  One step, stated exactly.
```

This skeleton outperforms polished brand-style commercials substantially. The five failure modes, in
order of how often they occur: opening with the brand, leading with features instead of the problem, a
vague CTA, running too long, and never testing hook variants.

## 4 · Script templates

**T1 · Proof by numbers** — the strongest fit for engineering work.
```
HOOK    [surprising number] in [time / comparison]
PROBLEM the old way costs [time/money] nobody sees
SOLUTION [project] does it by [method]
PROOF   from [X] to [Y], measured
CTA     [link] — or save this
```

**T2 · Failure story**
```
HOOK    I failed [N] times at [thing]
PROBLEM what I assumed was wrong ([first guess]) — it was not
SOLUTION what actually fixed it
PROOF   the result after the change
CTA     save this if you have been there
```

**T3 · One feature, screen-recorded**
```
HOOK    one feature, one problem it removes
PROBLEM how the old way is done
SOLUTION demo on SCREEN, not an animation
PROOF   the result at the end
CTA     the feature lives in [place]
```

**T4 · Before / after**
```
HOOK    it used to be like this, now like this
PROBLEM the old pain, concrete
SOLUTION what changed
PROOF   number before vs after
CTA     details at [place]
```

**T5 · How-to in 60 seconds**
```
HOOK    how to [outcome] in [time]
STEPS   three numbered steps, one sentence each
PROOF   the result after the last step
CTA     save it so you do not lose it
```

## 5 · Hook formula families

Mixing families is better than repeating one. Combine two triggers per hook.

- **Curiosity:** "Nobody tells you this about ___" · "I tested ___ for 30 days" · "Here is why ___ always
  fails" · "I was wrong about ___"
- **Recognition:** "POV: you are the one who ___" · "If you still ___, this is for you" · "Signs you
  already ___"
- **Surprise:** "Stop doing ___" · "___ turns out to be unnecessary" · "I removed ___ and it got better"
- **Hard numbers:** "[N] ___ in [time]" · "from [X] to [Y]" · "[N]% get this wrong" · "only [N] steps"
- **Contrarian:** "Everyone says ___. I disagree" · "___ is not the problem. This is" · "___ was never
  the expensive part"

## 6 · Concept formats worth borrowing

These describe shapes, not content — fill them from what genuinely exists.

- **Proof-by-numbers:** back a claim with hard figures instead of adjectives. Our equivalent raw material
  is repo counts, test counts, published API file counts.
- **Failure story:** show what went wrong and what it cost. Genuinely honest material outperforms a
  highlight reel, and we hold real material for it.
- **Rating hook:** "the habit I would score 10/10" — cheap to produce, works for advice pieces.
- **Hometown angle:** claim your own region rather than a famous one; travel and identity content.
- **Single unbroken take:** one shot, no cuts, reading as proof of authenticity. Needs a person on camera.

## 7 · Testing cadence and the numbers to watch

- The hook is the only variable worth optimising first; vary it before touching anything else.
- Run several variants per idea — roughly **5 hooks × 2 bodies × 2 CTAs**. The platform needs that many
  before a winner is statistically visible.
- Directional benchmarks for judging a result (target → strong):
  hook rate ~25% → 35–50% (Meta side), ~30% → 40–50% (shorter-video side); hold rate ~40% → 50–60%;
  completion ~60% → 80%+.
- Short-video sweet spot sits around 21–34 s for the most-viewed platform; under 90 s for the other.

## 8 · The tension with our own design rules

The platform evidence favours plain, authentic footage; this skill's design rules favour dense, designed
frames — and those rules exist because a plainly-built render was rejected outright by the owner.
So the two are not in direct conflict: **the design discipline stays, the question is what fills it.**
The untested hypothesis worth one comparison is a piece built on **real screen recordings of the actual
work** (a live terminal, a real dashboard, real data) with the voice-over on top, measured against the
animated format on the same script. Reproducible material for it may already be sitting in the owner's
output folder. Treat this as a documented open question, not as settled guidance.

## 9 · Sources and how much to trust them

Aggregated sources for the above, in decreasing strength: platform-side reporting on ranking factors and
format engagement (Meta-derived, third-party analyses of platform benchmarks); independent trend trackers
that publish weekly format round-ups; and **vendor marketing pages**, which are the weakest — several of
the funnel benchmarks in §7 come from vendors selling script tooling, so treat those figures as direction
rather than measurement. When the owner asks for recommendations, mark which claims are vendor-origin,
and state plainly that none of it could be verified against the account's own analytics.

Capture the sources' URLs in the deliverable so the next session can re-verify instead of re-searching.
